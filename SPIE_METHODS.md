## 2.1 Procedural anatomy generation

Anatomies are generated by perturbing a patient-derived arterial tree rather than by
sampling additional patient segmentations. The loaded tree comprises 25 centerline
branches; of these, only the navigated right common carotid (RCCA, total arclength
237.5 mm) and the proximal 35 mm of the adjacent right vertebral artery (RVA) are
modified. All remaining branches are passed through unchanged.

**Displacement field.** For each generation, the RCCA centerline is displaced by a sum of
three sinusoidal modes in normalised arclength *u* ∈ [0,1], with frequencies
(0.7, 1.3, 2.1) cycles. Each mode draws an independent amplitude *a* ∼ 𝒩(0,1) and phase
*p* ∼ 𝒰(0,2π), and modes are summed independently along two directions perpendicular to
the branch's overall chord, then normalised by √3. The displacement magnitude at a station
is therefore itself a random quantity whose *scale* is set by `base_amp_mm = 4.0 mm`
multiplied by a per-generation tortuosity factor τ ∼ clip(𝒩(1.0, 0.3), 0.4, 1.6).

**Envelope and anchoring.** The displacement is modulated by a bell envelope built from
two smoothstep ramps, *w* = min(*w*<sub>up</sub>, *w*<sub>down</sub>), which is zero over
the first 3 mm and the last 25 mm of the branch and unity through the middle (*w* = 1 for
*s* ∈ [18.0, 197.5] mm, ramp length 15 mm). The proximal anchor pins the RCCA ostium,
shared with the RVA and the parent bridge segment; the distal anchor pins the terminus and
its Circle-of-Willis junction. Because both ends are held fixed and the envelope is C¹,
the perturbed branch remains topologically connected to the unmodified tree and no surface
boolean operation is required. Lumen radii are scaled by the same envelope,
ρ ← ρ·(1 + (σ−1)·*w*) with σ ∼ max(0.75, 𝒩(1.0, 0.07)), so calibre varies mid-vessel while
both junctions retain their original radius.

**Fork randomisation.** The proximal 35 mm of the RVA receives an independent perturbation
(amplitude 3.0 mm, tapering to zero at 35 mm), so the geometry of the RCCA/RVA bifurcation
— and hence the deflection required to select the correct daughter — differs between
anatomies and cannot be memorised.

**Surface reconstruction.** The complete tree is re-meshed from centerlines at every
generation: spheres of the local radius are painted into a voxel grid of spacing
[0.6, 0.6, 0.9] mm, the volume is Gaussian-smoothed twice, an isosurface is extracted by
marching cubes, and the mesh is decimated.

**Clearance audit.** Reconstruction narrows the lumen, so each generated surface is audited
before use. Clearance is measured as the distance from each centerline station to the
nearest point *on the triangle surface* — sampled densely across faces, since
nearest-vertex distance overstates clearance on a coarse mesh. An anatomy is admitted only
if the minimum clearance exceeds the guidewire radius (0.18 mm) **and** the median
clearance is at least 2.00 mm; anatomies failing either test are redrawn. A calibration
sweep established that a radius scaling of 1.6 reproduces the patient-derived vessel's
clearance profile (median 2.14 mm vs 2.14 mm; 5th percentile 1.13 mm vs 1.20 mm, i.e.
marginally *tighter*). This ensures anatomical variation is not obtained by widening the
vessel — a generated anatomy is no easier to traverse than the patient's own.

**Anatomy streams.** Each of 16 parallel workers instantiates its own anatomy and redraws
it periodically, giving an estimated 700–800 distinct anatomies over a training run.

## 2.2 Simulation environment and control problem

**Simulation.** Finite-element simulation uses SOFA with the BeamAdapter plugin under a
`FreeMotionAnimationLoop` with Lagrangian constraint correction. The physics timestep is
6 ms and 22 substeps are integrated per control step, giving a control rate of 7.5 Hz.
Contact uses `LocalMinDistance` (contact distance 0.3 mm, alarm distance 0.5 mm) with a
Coulomb friction coefficient of 0.001.

**Devices.** A 0.36 mm J-tipped guidewire (tip radius 12.1 mm, tip arc 0.4π rad) and a
0.6/0.7 mm microcatheter, each 900 mm long and discretised into 553 beam elements, are
advanced coaxially. Both devices begin every episode at an independent uniform axial
rotation in [0, 2π), which alone can determine which daughter branch the J-tip enters.

**Action.** Four continuous commands — translation rate (±30 mm/s) and twist rate
(±1.5 rad/s) per device — matching the degrees of freedom a bench actuator drives, so a
learned policy is executable on hardware without re-parameterisation.

**Observations, and the privileged/deployable split.** The full observation is
125-dimensional and partitions into a **101-dimensional deployable prefix** and a
**24-dimensional privileged block**.

*Deployable* (obtainable in a catheterisation laboratory): projected device tracking (40;
ten points at 15 mm spacing, tip-relative, two-step history), target offset in the image
plane (2), previous command (4), planned-route guidance (51), and the scripted
controller's action for the current state (4). Forty of the 51 guidance dimensions derive
from the clinician's planned route — remaining distance, cross-track offset, heading
error, curvature ahead, distance and direction to the next correct daughter, and an
on-path indicator.

*Privileged* (simulator-only): nodal velocities and forces at the distal beam elements, a
contact-impulse proxy formed from the difference between constrained and free positions,
accumulated axial twist, and ground-truth branch identity. These are precisely the
quantities that mark stress states — buckling, wall loading, wind-up — and that are
unavailable to a fluoroscopic observer.

**Reward.** A weighted sum of: terminal target reward (+3.0 within 5 mm of the target);
dense arclength progress along the planned route (0.01 per mm, symmetric, so a round trip
nets zero) with a lateral-deviation penalty (0.001 per mm); a small per-step cost varying
by path segment (−0.007 to 0); discrete ±1.0 events on committing to a correct or
incorrect daughter at a fork; and a potential-based anti-buckling term (coefficient 0.5)
formed from guidewire slack and mean contact displacement. Episodes terminate on target
contact and truncate at 600 control steps or on simulator error; vessel-end truncation is
disabled so that off-path excursions remain recoverable rather than fatal.

**Residual composition.** The executed command is
*a*<sub>total</sub> = clip(*a*<sub>heuristic</sub> + *a*<sub>policy</sub>) in raw physical
units. The scripted controller is a parameterless centerline follower: it retracts when
off-route, otherwise corrects heading error and cross-track offset proportionally and
advances, with the catheter following at 0.8× the guidewire rate. The residual has unity
scale, so the network commands the full actuation range and can override the controller
entirely; at initialisation the system behaves exactly as the scripted controller, which
therefore serves as the study's baseline.

## 2.3 Privileged teacher

**Algorithm.** The teacher is trained with Soft Actor-Critic using two critics and Polyak
targets (τ = 0.005, γ = 0.99), taking min(*Q*₁, *Q*₂) in both the target and the actor
loss. The entropy term is omitted from the critic backup while retained in the actor
objective. Both networks are two-layer MLPs of width 256 without recurrence; LayerNorm is
applied in the critic bodies only. Optimisation uses Adam at 3×10⁻⁴ with gradient-norm
clipping at 1.0. The entropy temperature is auto-tuned against a target entropy of +1.0
(rather than the conventional −*n*<sub>actions</sub>) with log α railed to [−5, 0]; the
policy log-standard-deviation is soft-bounded to σ ∈ (0.135, 1.0).

**Why not an advantage-weighted objective.** An advantage-weighted variant (AWAC) was used
in an earlier generation of this work and abandoned. Its exponential advantage weights
collapsed toward unity, degenerating the update into near-uniform behaviour cloning of
whatever the buffer contained — including the policy's own increasingly extreme successes,
a positive feedback loop with no counterweight. Entropy-regularised SAC with a raised
target entropy was adopted instead, retaining an explicit exploration pressure that the
advantage-weighted objective lacks. This matters here because the recovery behaviour of
§2.6 is rare in the buffer and is precisely what an objective that clones the buffer's
modal behaviour would discard.

**Replay.** A 2×10⁶-transition buffer with prioritised sampling (exponent 0.6,
importance-sampling exponent annealed 0.4 → 1.0) and batch size 256. Thirty percent of each
batch is drawn from a second tree restricted to episodes that reached the correct daughter
branch. There is no offline data, demonstration seeding, or pretraining: the buffer is
filled purely online after a 20,000-step warm-up at reduced action scale.

**Scale.** Sixteen CPU workers feed one GPU trainer at a measured 10–11 environment steps
per second, with an update-to-data ratio of 0.99. The teacher was trained for 2.0×10⁶
environment steps.

The teacher observes all 125 dimensions and is therefore **not deployable**; it exists to
generate supervision for the student of §2.5.

## 2.4 Correspondence between planned-path geometry and privileged state

The distillation of §2.5 is only viable if the deployable observation carries information
about the stress states the teacher conditions on. We test this directly rather than
assume it.

A probe is trained to predict privileged stress quantities — contact-impulse magnitude and
distal nodal force — from the deployable planned-path features alone, on states sampled
from teacher rollouts across audited anatomies. Performance is reported as area under the
ROC curve for a binarised high-contact state and as coefficient of determination for the
continuous targets, against a shuffled-label control.

Two constraints make the test meaningful. First, any deployable feature that is
**numerically identical** to a privileged dimension is excluded from the probe: guidewire
slack, in particular, appears on both sides of the split, computed from the same path
projection, and including it would measure an identity rather than a correspondence.
Second, the probe is fitted and evaluated on **audited anatomies only**, since geometry
that the device cannot traverse produces contact signatures that no deployable feature
should be expected to explain.

The result establishes the extent to which planned-path geometry acts as an observable
proxy for unobservable mechanical state, and therefore bounds what distillation can
recover. ⟨**PENDING** — see inventory H1–H3⟩

## 2.5 Student distillation

The deployable controller is obtained by distilling the teacher into a student restricted
to the 101-dimensional deployable prefix, using DAgger. The student is initialised by
regression to the teacher's actions on teacher-generated states, then iteratively: the
student is rolled out in the environment; the teacher — which retains access to privileged
state at every visited configuration — labels each visited state with the action it would
have taken; and the student is updated by regression toward those labels on the aggregated
dataset. Iterating on states the *student* visits, rather than only those the teacher
visits, corrects the compounding distribution shift that pure behaviour cloning suffers
when the student's errors carry it into configurations the teacher never entered.

The student shares the teacher's architecture apart from the input width and is composed
with the same scripted controller by the same residual rule, so teacher and student differ
only in what they observe. All reported deployable results are the student's; the teacher's
score is reported alongside as an upper bound on what distillation could recover.

## 2.6 Behavioural analysis

Navigation performance is decomposed into two behaviours by post-hoc analysis of the
complete step logs. A **stall** opens when the guidewire is advanced above 2 mm/s while
path progress remains flat within 0.3 mm for twelve consecutive steps, using a decaying
counter so isolated command reversals do not close it. A stall resolves when progress
passes the frontier at which it began, and is classified by the guidewire length actually
withdrawn — under 1 mm, 1–8 mm, or over 8 mm — measured from executed insertion rather than
commanded retraction, since a retraction command against a wedged device moves nothing.

Three success measures are separated because they occupy different units: episode success
(per episode), stall-resolution rate (per stall event), and the conversion of resolved
stalls into successful episodes (per episode). Absolute rates move by roughly 20 percentage
points across reasonable threshold choices, so only differences and trends are reported,
and the analysis is restricted to audited anatomies. ⟨**PENDING** re-analysis — inventory B1⟩

## 2.7 Evaluation protocol

Evaluation uses 98 fixed seeds across 50 procedurally generated anatomies, redrawn every
two episodes across 16 workers, with the clearance audit of §2.1 applied at generation
time. Actions are taken deterministically as tanh of the policy mean. An episode succeeds
if the guidewire tip comes within 5 mm (3-D Euclidean) of a target sampled at least 40 mm
into the RCCA, within 600 control steps.

Anatomy identity is established by hashing branch-coordinate arrays. This was necessary
because the obvious identifiers are unreliable: the planned path moves with the target, and
the mesh fingerprint is built from a seed reassigned on reset, so both can report a match
between geometrically different vessels.

Results are stratified by planned-path length into three bands with cut points at 146 mm and
210 mm, corresponding approximately to the common carotid, the mid-internal carotid, and the
cavernous segment. These are cut-points on path length rather than anatomical segmentation;
the modelled vessel terminates at the ICA terminus and the middle cerebral artery is not
represented.

Transfer is assessed on the patient-derived anatomy using its **original segmented surface**
rather than a reconstruction, with all other settings unchanged.

Binomial (Wilson) confidence intervals are reported. These understate the true uncertainty:
episodes are not independent — 16 persistent simulator processes each run 6–7 sequential
episodes — and initial device twist is unseeded, so repeat launches are not bit-reproducible.
