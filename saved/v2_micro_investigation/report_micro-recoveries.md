## verdict
Micro-recoveries (stall->retract->resume) do occur inside successful episodes — ~53% of explore successes contain at least one full cycle under the tier-2 "deliberate retract" detector, 329 of those cycles start with the wire already in RCCA (146/420 = 35% of successes contain an RCCA micro-recovery, the user's exact scenario), and one captured deterministic-eval success shows a textbook noise-free RCCA cycle (stall onset step 337 with buckle engaged at -0.025, retract latency 6 steps, resumed by step 349, episode succeeded). BUT two crucial qualifications: (1) in explore data the retract criterion is noise-saturated — Gaussian action noise (median per-step |diff a0| 15-30 mm/s, di0 noise ~1.7mm/step) makes the specified -1.5mm-net criterion fire in 100% of control windows and even the deliberate tier-2 criterion in 94% — so most explore "retracts" are exploration noise, and the honest measurement of the LEARNED policy comes from the deterministic eval window: there, mild retraction after stalls is genuine but weak (tier1 90% of stalls, tier2 48%, strong pull-backs tier3 only 7%; median latency ~20 steps; the net post-stall response is still +13.2mm INSERTED over 20 steps). (2) The behavior is NOT being learned/increasing — it is fading: full-cycle prevalence inside successes falls 86%->58%->28% across training thirds, post-stall 20-step mean a0 intent hardens from +1.8 to +7.2 mm/s, and the fraction of stalls answered with net-negative intent drops 30%->19%->13%. The policy is learning stall AVOIDANCE (success stalls/100 steps 1.22->0.64) while its stall RESPONSE converges toward push-harder-forward. Retract-when-stuck is also not the success discriminator: failures retract-and-resume MORE than successes (mean 3.8 vs 1.4 full cycles/ep; 87% vs 53% any-cycle) — what separates successes is not stalling at all and stalling without slack (buckle ~0 vs -0.19 at failure stalls).

## evidence
Method: parsed steps.tsv (635,751 rows) + outcomes.tsv (1,339 eps); segmented per-pid episode instances on ep-change/ep_step-reset and joined Nth-occurrence chronologically (95.3% exact length match, 94.3% final-branch match; 1,276 validated episodes used). Regime classification via per-episode median |diff| of rotation dims a1/a3 (explore noise ~0.7-1.6 vs deterministic <0.5): identified 72 scripted-heatup eps at run start (jitter ~0.003, a1/a3 frozen), a 67-ep deterministic eval block at cum-steps 322k-358k (= explore ~279-315k, matching eval1 @287k), 16 stray deterministic eps late in run (consistent with tanh-railed/entropy-collapsed episodes), 1,121 explore eps. Ran three retract-detector tiers plus a control-window false-positive baseline (detector applied after freely-progressing onsets) — this exposed that the specified -1.5mm criterion cannot distinguish policy retraction from exploration noise (100% FP), so learned behavior was measured two noise-robust ways: (a) the deterministic eval block (no sampling noise: every detected retract is genuine policy output) and (b) population-level post-stall intent (mean a0 over 20 steps after stall onset, noise averages out over 375-1,700 stalls per cell). Strongest observations: eval policy retracts mildly after 90% (T1) / 48% (T2) of stalls but nearly never strongly (T3 7%) and still nets +13mm forward in 20 steps post-stall; explore post-stall intent monotonically hardens +1.8->+7.2 mm/s across thirds while %negative-intent stalls halves; success stall rate falls 1.22->0.64 per 100 steps as success rises 29->44%; buckle is engaged 4-7x background exactly at eval/failure retract sites but is ~0 at success stalls; and the single captured eval success with an RCCA stall executed a clean 6-step-latency retract-resume cycle.

## implications
(1) Micro-recovery capability exists in the policy but is weak, slow (~20-step latency), shallow (~1.5-2mm backouts, T3 pull-backs 7%), and FADING — it was almost certainly distilled from exploration-noise demonstrations (noise produces -1.5mm dips everywhere, AWAC clones the successful ones), and with alpha pinned at its 0.1 ceiling and entropy collapsing, that demonstration source is drying up while post-stall push-forward intent hardens. If you want retract-when-stuck as a durable skill for the next iteration, do not rely on entropy: either raise the alpha ceiling / add an entropy floor specifically to keep a0 exploration alive, or inject explicit retract-jiggle segments into heatup/harvest (scripted stall->retract 3-5mm->re-advance demonstrations, especially at the RCCA ostium where 82% of stalls happen), or add an advantage/reward term for negative di0 conditioned on the online-detectable stall signature (proj flat over 10 steps + buckle<=-0.05 — buckle_phi is already a reliable stall marker, engaged 4-7x background at genuine stall sites). (2) The held-out eval gap is a recovery gap: eval failures are stuck-grinders (69% stall, only 33% cycle-through, 97% of post-stall intent still forward) — improving stall response on held-out meshes is the highest-leverage path from 30.6% eval toward explore's 44%. (3) Stall avoidance, not recovery, drove explore gains; these are different skills and current training is trading the second for the first. (4) For any future analysis of this run: never use raw retract-event counts on explore data (100% noise FP) — use deterministic snapshots/eval windows or population intent stats; and note eval1's logged subset here showed 13.4% success (9/67) vs the reported 30.6%, so recompute eval metrics from the run's own eval logger, not the steplog.

## caveats
(1) Explore event-level cycle counts (class-table T1/T2 columns for non-eval rows) are noise-saturated upper bounds — the control FP rate is 94-100% — so "successes contain micro-recoveries" at the event level is confirmed only directionally (eval window + the one noise-free RCCA success) and prevalence magnitudes for explore are not trustworthy; the SUCC<FAIL ordering and all trend directions are robust across the W/RESUME sensitivity grid. (2) The eval block captured only 67 of ~98 eval episodes (join dropped 63/1339 eps overall) and its logged success rate (13.4%) disagrees with the reported 30.6% — eval-window retract stats may be biased toward failure-heavy episodes; alternative reading: some captured "eval" 600-step eps could be railed-deterministic explore eps, though the tight wall-time block at exactly explore ~287k argues against. (3) The declining cycle prevalence inside successes is partly compositional: as training progresses successes are increasingly episodes that never stall, so per-episode cycle counts fall even if per-stall response were constant — the intent trend (+1.8->+7.2 mm/s, %negative-intent 30->13%) is the cleaner evidence that the response itself is hardening, but it too mixes state distribution shift (later stalls may be different/harder). (4) proj_s projection ambiguity can create spurious stall onsets and the RESUME (+2mm over pre-stall max) cannot be satisfied near path end, slightly deflating failure resume rates. (5) 'RVA-dip' used off_br>0 mid-episode as the proxy for touching the wrong branch; 38/58 such successes were still flagged is_clean=1, implying is_clean thresholds differ from any-off_br — the two overlap but are not nested as assumed. (6) Thirds are by wall time over the extract (roughly explore 43k-445k), not by update step; heatup (72 scripted eps) and 16 stray deterministic eps were excluded from all explore/trend numbers.
