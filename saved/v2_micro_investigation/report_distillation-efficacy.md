## verdict
Partial transfer of inference, no transfer to behavior. The aux distillation DID put contact information into the policy trunk — the aux head predicts the true privileged contact proxy at r≈0.71-0.75 (R²≈0.55 peak), far above what deployable gw_slack alone yields (R²=0.002) — but (1) half the aux targets are dead: the SOFA force labels (flat 99/100, rel 2/3) are identically zero across all 568,874 buffer states (exception-guarded accessor returning 0), so the head just learned to output a constant for them; (2) the live contact labels have std ~1e-3 in normalized units, making aux_coef*MSE ~5e-8 — essentially zero shaping pressure on the shared representation; and (3) critically, the inference does NOT drive retraction: P(retract) is flat (~0.40-0.45) across all contact quintiles at the latest snapshot, the weak correct-signed contact coupling present at pretrain (0.109→0.164 across quintiles) VANISHED with training, corr(a0, policy's own aux contact prediction) is POSITIVE (+0.12 to +0.17 — it pushes harder when it infers more contact), and the only correctly-signed behavioral coupling (a +7-13pp retract bump in the high-slack/buckled tail) is weakening, not steepening (u=131k: 0.510 vs 0.381 base; u=246k: 0.448 vs 0.366). Root cause is on the credit-assignment side: the critic does hold a correctly-signed retract-when-stuck preference (AWAC weight for retract vs advance actions in the buckled-slack tail: 1.042 vs 0.947), but with advantage std 0.092 and awac_lambda=1.0 the AWAC weights span only ~[0.72, 1.25] (1-99th pct) — the update is near-uniform behavior cloning that cannot transmit that preference to the policy.

## implications
Priority 1 — fix the dead privileged force channels: rel dims 2,3,4 (flat 99-101) are identically zero over 569k states; the SOFA dofs.force accessor silently fails (exception-guarded to 0). Either repair it or re-point aux_labels at live dims (0,1 velocities + 5,6 contact). Half the current aux capacity trains on constants. Priority 2 — rescale aux labels to unit variance: contact labels have std 9.4e-4/5.7e-3, so aux_coef(0.05)*MSE ~1e-8 — vanishing gradient on the shared trunk; z-score the labels (running stats) or tighten the normalizers (e.g. /0.25mm and /1mm instead of /2mm and /10mm) and retune aux_coef so the aux term is ~1-10% of policy loss. Priority 3 (biggest lever) — the failure is credit assignment, not inference: with adv std 0.092 and awac_lambda=1.0, AWAC degenerates to uniform BC (weight IQR 1.07x), so the critic's correctly-signed retract-when-buckled preference (10% relative weight edge in the slack tail) transmits nothing; lower awac_lambda to ~0.1-0.3 or normalize advantages (adv/std) before exp, targeting a p99/p1 weight ratio of ~5-20x, and monitor the existing awac_weight_saturation metric from the other side (it currently reads 0.000 = under-spread, not collapse). Priority 4 — stuck states are rare in the buffer (top slack tail ~10%, top contact ~1%), so even a fixed weighting gives them little gradient share: add stuckness-balanced sampling (analogous to the existing balanced_fraction on is_clean) keyed on slack/contact tails, and consider an explicit reward term for retraction-that-reduces-slack since buckle_reward_coef=0.5 alone has not produced state-conditioned retraction. Priority 5 — the policy's contact inference (r~0.75) is real but currently drives ADVANCE (+0.12 corr with a0); once retraction is actually reinforced in stuck states (via 3+4), re-run this exact probe: the diagnostic signature of success is the contact-bin P(retract) gradient reappearing and steepening from u~130k onward instead of flattening.

## caveats
(1) "Earliest" snapshot u=10,000 is post-pretrain (10k updates on heatup data), not random init — pretrain-time coupling already reflects learning; no true-init reference exists. (2) The buffer contains ~282k heatup transitions that are NOT flagged (is_demo=0 for the entire buffer, though explore was only 287k at save), so all tables mix heatup-visited and on-policy states; conditioning is on state so conclusions stand, but bin populations are not pure on-policy, and the "explore-only" split in my first pass was vacuous. (3) All R²/behavior is evaluated on the buffer frozen at explore 287k; late snapshots (e=399-511k) are evaluated slightly off their newest data distribution, which could understate late R² a little (no eviction had occurred — capacity 2M — so their older training data is included). (4) The AWAC reproduction used deterministic tanh(mu) for V(s) where training uses a sampled action — adds noise to per-state advantage but cannot change the order of magnitude of the 0.092 spread; entropy term and PER IS-weights were also omitted from the reproduction. (5) High contact/slack are imperfect stuckness proxies — high contact also occurs during normal deep tortuous navigation where advancing is correct, so the advance response in top contact bins is suggestive rather than damning; the slack-tail (buckle) test is cleaner and shows the weak, non-steepening coupling. (6) Negative-slack semantics (68% of states; tip arclength projection exceeding inserted length) are unclear — the U-shaped retract pattern across slack may partly reflect projection artifacts. (7) P(retract) uses the deterministic action at threshold -1mm/s; rollout behavior adds sampling noise (log_std band cap 0.0) around these means. (8) R²/Pearson computed on one 65,536-state sample (seed 0); values are stable at this N but carry ~±0.01 sampling error.
