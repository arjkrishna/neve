## verdict
Replace AWAC's transmission mechanism (advantage-weighted BC) rather than keep re-tuning its brakes. The post-2023 literature has largely abandoned the policy-constraint family for offline-to-online finetuning precisely because of the failure you documented: the constraint anchors the actor to a unimodal BC target and the advantage signal degenerates. The best-evidenced, lowest-effort replacement on your stack is RLPD (Ball/Smith/Kostrikov/Levine, ICML 2023): pure SAC with 50/50 symmetric sampling of your 280k demo buffer vs online replay, LayerNorm critics, critic-subset ensembling, high UTD, and NO BC term — this deletes the advantage-weight-collapse pathology by construction, and its LayerNorm+ensemble recipe is the documented cure for the value-explosion side of your instability; it reaches SOTA on sparse Adroit/AntMaze within ~300k online steps, comfortably inside 1-2M steps/day. RLPD alone does NOT fix the bimodal-recovery pathology (it keeps a tanh-Gaussian actor), so the second structural change should be the policy class: flow-matching one-step policies with chunked actions — QC-FQL (Q-chunking, Li/Zhou/Levine NeurIPS 2025, built on FQL, Park/Li/Levine ICML 2025) — which represents the bimodal commit-vs-retract distribution natively and turns the temporally-extended retract-then-re-advance maneuver into a single chunked macro-action with unbiased n-step backups, while deploying as a single deterministic forward pass at 7.5Hz. A lower-risk intermediate that keeps your SAC code is DSRL (Wagenmaker et al., CoRL 2025): freeze a BC flow/diffusion policy trained on demos+successes and run SAC in its latent-noise space, so the frozen policy carries the bimodal recovery modes and the RL head only selects among them. JSRL-style roll-in with your scripted heuristic is a free, orthogonal collection-side add-on. Model-based (TD-MPC2 + MoDem-style demo seeding) is the fallback if model-free plateaus; on-policy PPO+domain-randomization is arithmetically out of reach on SOFA throughput.

## candidates
RANKED (stability gain x fit / adoption effort):

1. RLPD — "Efficient Online Reinforcement Learning with Offline Data" (Ball, Smith, Kostrikov, Levine, ICML 2023, arXiv:2302.02948). Mechanism: standard SAC where every gradient batch is 50% offline-buffer / 50% online-replay ("symmetric sampling"), LayerNorm on critics (ablation: removing LayerNorm collapses performance to zero on restricted expert-only sparse Adroit — it bounds Q-extrapolation), ensemble of ~10 critics with min over a random subset (num_min_qs=2), UTD~20, entropy backup optionally disabled on sparse tasks; no BC/constraint term at all. Reported ~2.5x improvement over prior methods across 21 benchmarks, converging in ~300k online steps. FIT: directly deletes pathology (1) — there is no advantage weight to collapse; the critic's retract-when-stuck preference transmits via plain Q-maximization on half-offline batches. LayerNorm+ensemble attacks the value-side driver of pathology (2). Budget: 300k-1M online steps is about one day of SOFA. Assets: the 280k buffer becomes the offline lane — your PER "balanced lanes" already implement symmetric sampling structurally; asymmetric privileged critic fully compatible; 16 workers unchanged; deterministic tanh-mean deploy unchanged. EFFORT: LOW (days, config-level change on the existing SAC/AWAC trainer). RISK: keeps the tanh-Gaussian actor, so sigma/alpha dynamics (pathology 2) and bimodal averaging (pathology 4) are mitigated, not removed — adopt their recipe (entropy backup off) rather than the action-mean-penalty knife-edge; high UTD raises GPU cost (not SOFA cost).

2. QC-FQL / Q-chunking — "Reinforcement Learning with Action Chunking" (Li, Zhou, Levine, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2507.07969) on "Flow Q-Learning" (Park, Li, Levine, ICML 2025). Mechanism: run TD-based RL directly in a chunked action space (actor emits h actions, critic values the chunk, unbiased n-step backups), with an expressive flow-matching BC policy as behavior prior; FQL trains a ONE-STEP policy distilled from the BC flow to maximize Q — no iterative denoising at deployment, no recursive backprop through the sampler. Explicitly offline-to-online; outperforms prior best offline-to-online methods (incl. n-step baselines) on long-horizon sparse-reward manipulation. FIT: the only family that structurally attacks pathologies (3) and (4): the flow policy is multimodal (commit-vs-retract both representable, no Gaussian averaging), and stall-retract-re-advance becomes ONE chunked action that is Q-valued and committed to, instead of a fragile sequence exploration noise must rediscover — recovery no longer fades as entropy declines. Deployment: single forward pass, deterministic via fixed noise or best-of-N argmax-Q over 4-dim actions, trivially fast at 7.5Hz. Assets: 280k buffer re-chunked into h-step sequences; recovery-friendly episodes are exactly the temporally coherent data chunking exploits. EFFORT: MEDIUM-HIGH (new actor class + chunked replay; official JAX repo, porting to your torch stack is real work). RISK: open-loop chunk execution during contact-rich FEM buckling reduces reactivity — keep h small (3-5 steps, ~0.4-0.7s at 7.5Hz); chunking changes PER bookkeeping.

3. DSRL — "Steering Your Diffusion Policy with Latent Space Reinforcement Learning" (Wagenmaker et al., CoRL 2025, arXiv:2506.15799). Mechanism: train a BC diffusion/flow policy on demos+successes, FREEZE it, train a small SAC agent (~500k params) over its latent-noise input; the latent policy selects which mode of the frozen multimodal policy to express. Demonstrated highly sample-efficient real-world robot improvement, incl. steering a frozen pi0. FIT: solves the bimodal pathology with minimal disruption — your SAC/replay/16-worker infra trains the latent policy unchanged; the frozen base carries retract-vs-commit modes learned from heuristic + noise-demonstration data; deterministic deploy = frozen policy + deterministic latent mean; the tanh-Gaussian instability surface shrinks because the base policy bounds behavior. EFFORT: MEDIUM. RISK: ceiling limited by base-policy support — if clean recovery maneuvers are rare in the buffer, steerable modes are weak; periodic re-distillation of the base from new successes mitigates.

4. JSRL — "Jump-Start Reinforcement Learning" (Uchendu et al., ICML 2023, arXiv:2204.02372). Mechanism: guide-policy rolls in for the first h steps, exploration policy completes the episode; anneal h. Provably improves sample complexity from exponential to polynomial in horizon for non-optimistic methods. FIT: maps one-to-one onto your scripted heuristic + restore-from-z curriculum (you have hand-built proto-JSRL); zero learner changes; stacks with candidates 1-3. EFFORT: VERY LOW. RISK: fixes no actor pathology by itself — add-on, not replacement.

5. Cal-QL / WSRL (value-calibration family) — "Cal-QL: Calibrated Offline RL Pre-Training for Efficient Online Fine-Tuning" (Nakamoto et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2303.05479); "Efficient Online RL Fine-Tuning Need Not Retain Offline Data" (Zhou et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv:2412.07762). Mechanism: Cal-QL bounds conservative Q below the learned-policy value but above a reference policy so online finetuning does not unlearn (9/11 benchmark wins, one-line change on CQL); WSRL shows a short warmup of frozen-policy rollouts recalibrates Q, after which unconstrained high-UTD online RL beats data-retaining methods. FIT: high diagnostic value — they name your freeze pathology as the known constraint/pessimism boundary failure — but Cal-QL requires adopting CQL machinery (new tuning burden, still Gaussian actor), and WSRL's insight is subsumed by RLPD on your infra. The "Three Regimes of Offline-to-Online RL" study (arXiv:2510.01460, 2025, fetched in full) predicts method choice from pretrained-policy-vs-data quality with 71% accuracy; in your hard regions the pretrained policy is comparable/inferior to buffer quality, and its data favors D-centric methods (offline-data replay + resets, i.e., the RLPD/WSRL family: 83% success in the inferior regime) over pi0-centric constraint methods — independent support for ranking RLPD above AWAC-likes. EFFORT: MEDIUM. Ranked lower: fixes only pathology (1)'s cousin, not (2)-(4).

6. TD-MPC2 + MoDem-style demo seeding — "TD-MPC2: Scalable, Robust World Models for Continuous Control" (Hansen, Su, Wang, ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.16828); "MoDem" (Hansen et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv:2212.05698). Mechanism: decoder-free latent world model + short-horizon MPPI planning + TD learning; beats SAC and DreamerV3 in data efficiency across 104 tasks with one hyperparameter set; MoDem shows demo oversampling + policy pretraining + targeted exploration make demo-seeded MBRL work on sparse tasks. FIT: 121-dim state obs is in its wheelhouse; sample efficiency fits budget; planning can natively choose "retract now" if the model predicts the stall. EFFORT: HIGH (full swap of trainer + replay plumbing). RISK: the world model must capture friction/buckling/slack FEM dynamics — exactly where compounding model error is worst; MPPI deploy is stochastic (needs argmax/seed tricks for determinism) and planning latency at 7.5Hz must be validated. Fallback tier.

7. IDQL / Diffusion-QL — "IDQL: Implicit Q-Learning as an Actor-Critic Method with Diffusion Policies" (Hansen-Estruch, Kostrikov, Janner, Kuba, Levine, 2023, arXiv:2304.10573); "Diffusion Policies as an Expressive Policy Class for Offline RL" (Wang et al., ICLR 2023). Mechanism: IQL critic + diffusion actor; actions extracted by sampling N candidates and argmaxing Q. FIT: your existing IQL scaffold makes this the cheapest expressive-actor path, and N-sample argmax over 4-dim actions is cheap and deterministic-given-seed at 7.5Hz; but 2025 evidence favors FQL/QC (one-step distillation avoids slow sampling in the training loop). Stepping stone, superseded by candidate 2.

8. Distributional critics (adjunct, not a family) — e.g., CTD4 (arXiv:2405.02576, 2024), distorted-distributional offline evaluation (2026). Quantile heads allow non-uniform pessimism (pessimistic tail for OOD, median for improvement), plausibly decoupling pessimism from freeze. Evidence in offline-to-online specifically is thinner than for LayerNorm+ensemble, which RLPD shows covers most of the same stability ground. Consider only as a cheap bolt-on to candidate 1.

## anti_recommendations
(a) PPO + asymmetric critic + massive domain randomization (the legged-locomotion recipe, Rudin et al. CoRL 2022): looks applicable because you already have an asymmetric critic, procedural anatomy variation, and parallel workers — but the recipe's engine is throughput (4096+ envs, tens of millions to billions of steps in minutes); at 1-2M SOFA steps/day with 16 workers you are 2-3 orders of magnitude short, and PPO discards your replay buffer, PER lanes, and 280k demo asset. Only revisit if a learned surrogate simulator replaces SOFA for the inner loop. (b) DPPO as the route to a diffusion policy: excellent stability story, but it is on-policy PPO fine-tuning demonstrated on cheap parallelized sims; under SOFA cost it burns your budget on gradient variance reduction that off-policy FQL/DSRL get from replay. (c) Vanilla Diffusion Policy / ACT / BeT / VQ-BeT as pure imitation: they fix the bimodality representation but have no online-improvement mechanism, so they inherit the heuristic's 30-100% region-dependent ceiling; and multi-step DDPM sampling adds latency you would then need consistency-distillation (Prasad et al. 2024) to remove — use their ideas via FQL/QC instead, which come with the RL loop built in. (d) Staying in the policy-constraint family with a different constraint (TD3+BC, SPOT, CRR, or AWAC-with-better-lambda): these share AWAC's structural failure — an explicit/implicit BC anchor toward a unimodal Gaussian fit of mixed-quality data; the Three Regimes analysis shows pi0/constraint-centric methods systematically underperform in exactly your regime, so more knob-tuning (the 0.005-vs-0.02 action-mean-penalty knife-edge) is fighting the class, not the tuning. (e) DreamerV3: designed for from-scratch online learning with reconstruction-based world models; no natural demo-buffer integration path (MoDem/TD-MPC2 is the demo-seeded MBRL line), and TD-MPC2 dominates it on data efficiency in continuous control anyway. (f) Cal-QL as a first move: adopting CQL machinery to fix a pathology that RLPD dissolves for free on your existing infrastructure is poor effort allocation — keep it as a diagnosis, not a destination.

## caveats
(1) Several claims rest on abstracts, project pages, and GitHub configs rather than full-PDF reads: RLPD's exact ensemble size/symmetric-sampling ratio came from the repo's script strings plus secondary summaries (the arXiv abstract fetch returned metadata only); Q-chunking's chunk length h and exact benchmark numbers were not verifiable from the pages fetched (repo default is believed to be h~5 but unconfirmed — check github.com/ColinQiyangLi/qc before committing). (2) No paper tests any of these algorithms on FEM-based endovascular navigation; the closest domain work (Karstensen/Robertshaw line) still uses SAC/LSTM or IRL, so transfer of RLPD/QC-FQL results from Adroit/AntMaze/robomimic to wire-buckling contact dynamics is an extrapolation — the bimodal-at-stall structure argument for expressive policies is mechanistic reasoning, not benchmarked evidence. (3) The 2602.20216 domain paper (online expert correction for catheter steering) could not be parsed in detail from the PDF fetch; it may contain directly relevant sample-budget numbers worth a manual read. (4) Deterministic deployment of flow/diffusion policies requires a design decision (fixed noise seed vs best-of-N argmax-Q); argmax-Q deployment reintroduces critic error at eval time and none of the fetched sources quantify this gap. (5) RLPD's high-UTD recipe assumes gradient steps are cheap relative to env steps — true for you (SOFA-bound), but interaction with your PER-lane prioritization is untested; RLPD uses uniform sampling within each half, and naively composing PER with symmetric sampling is unstudied. (6) The Three Regimes paper is recent (2025, v3 2026) and not yet widely replicated; its regime taxonomy supports but should not solely drive the choice. (7) Distributional-critic evidence for the specific pessimism-freeze coupling is indirect (no direct offline-to-online ablation found in the fetched sources).
