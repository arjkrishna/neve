## verdict
You are already AT or slightly ABOVE published domain SOTA for the hardest version of your task (two coaxial devices in high-tortuosity anatomy). The best public two-device / bifurcation-in-tortuosity numbers are: stEVE DualDeviceNav = 40/100 (Karstensen/Robertshaw et al., the exact framework you run), SAC-EIL-GAIL = 59% (Feb 2026, sample-efficient expert-in-the-loop), SplineFormer = 50% real-robot single-branch cannulation. NO published system demonstrates clean high-success siphon-level (multiple ~180 deg bends) two-device autonomy with explicit slack/buckling recovery. So your ~50% "stuck-grinder ceiling" is the field frontier, not a local bug. The systems that outperform naive SAC do FOUR things you are not doing, and none of them rely on a better-tuned unimodal tanh-Gaussian: (1) they exploit demonstrations/heuristics INTERACTIVELY at the states the deterministic policy actually visits (DAgger / HG-DAgger / online expert correction), not just as a static AWAC/BC prior; (2) they explicitly collect and train on RECOVERY demonstrations (the strongest recent result trained on 429 recovery vs 218 normal demos); (3) their deploy-time policy is MULTIMODAL and temporally chunked (ACT/CVAE, diffusion, B-spline transformer) rather than single-step Gaussian, which is the literature-backed fix for both your bimodal commit-vs-retract pathology and your tanh-saturation instability; (4) offline/critic winners use CONSERVATIVE critics (CQL/CASOG), not advantage-weighted BC — CASOG explicitly cures the Q-overestimation/distribution-shift that shows up in your run as advantage collapse. Buckling/stall-recovery is almost never handled inside the RL policy in this field; it lives in classical buckling-detect->retract safety reflexes, colonoscopy loop-reduction / follow-the-leader, and needle-steering retraction primitives. Your framing of slack/buckling as the CORE learning problem is actually ahead of most of the endovascular-RL literature — which means the transferable ideas come from adjacent domains, not from your direct competitors.

## candidates
RANKED BY FIT-TO-YOUR-CONSTRAINTS:

1) DAgger / online expert-in-the-loop correction using YOUR scripted heuristic as the interactive expert. Papers: "Sample-Efficient Learning with Online Expert Correction for Autonomous Catheter Steering in Endovascular Bifurcation Navigation" (arXiv:2602.20216, Feb 2026) — SAC+GAIL+Expert-in-the-Loop fuzzy correction at bifurcations, sigmoid reward scheduling; converged in 123 episodes vs 166 for SAC, 59% vs 44.67% success. "Safe Navigation for Robotic Digestive Endoscopy via Human Intervention-based RL" (arXiv:2409.15688, 2024). MECHANISM: at states where the deterministic policy stalls/grinds, query the heuristic (or a stall-detector -> retract macro) for the correct action, aggregate into the buffer, retrain — directly fixing covariate shift on the exact recovery states a shrinking-entropy Gaussian never samples. FIT: extremely sample-frugal (reuses your 16 SOFA workers + 280k buffer + heuristic that already scores 30-100%); attacks the "micro-recovery only from exploration noise" pathology head-on because recovery becomes labeled data, not noise; deterministic-deploy safe (BC-style target). EFFORT: low-medium (you already have the expert and the buffer plumbing). RISK: heuristic is imperfect in siphon (low-region), so DAgger inherits its ceiling there — mitigate by teleop/privileged-planner labels in the worst region.

2) Multimodal deploy-time policy (action-chunking transformer / diffusion / mixture) to replace the tanh-Gaussian. Papers: "Autonomous Soft Robotic Guidewire Navigation via Imitation Learning" (arXiv:2510.09497, 2025) — Surgical-Robot-Transformer-style action-chunking CVAE, RELATIVE action outputs to absorb hysteresis, trained with DAgger recovery demos (429 recovery / 218 normal), 83% on UNSEEN geometries; explicitly chose IL over RL because "RL... significantly worse on physical platforms." ACT (Zhao et al. 2023, CVAE+transformer). Diffusion-policy line and "Push Smarter Not Harder: HeRD" (arXiv:2512.10099, 2025) use diffusion at the low level precisely because a unimodal Gaussian "would lack this behavioral richness." SplineFormer (Huang et al., IROS 2025, arXiv:2501.04515) B-spline transformer. MECHANISM: a CVAE/diffusion head represents the BIMODAL stall distribution (commit vs retract) without averaging; action chunking spans the non-Markovian retract-then-readvance maneuver; relative actions handle windup/hysteresis. FIT: directly kills pathologies (2)+(4); deterministic deploy via temporal-ensemble (ACT) or few-step DDIM with fixed seed; consumes your 280k demos + heuristic rollouts as training data. EFFORT: medium (new policy head; can keep asymmetric critic). RISK: needs enough recovery-labeled chunks; pure BC of a mediocre heuristic caps quality — pair with candidate 1 or 3.

3) Conservative offline critic (CQL/CASOG) instead of AWAC advantage weighting. Paper: "CASOG: Conservative Actor-critic with SmOoth Gradient for Skill Learning in Robot-Assisted Intervention" (Zhou et al., IEEE TMRB 2023, arXiv:2304.09632) — conservatively estimates Q + smooths conv gradients to fight distribution shift/overfitting; TD-error-prioritized sampling to focus on hard manipulations; 94% guidewire delivery, and it explicitly MEASURES retraction ("mean backward steps 14.07"), performing closer to humans than prior offline RL. MECHANISM: your documented advantage-collapse-to-1.0 is the AWAC symptom of an unreliable Q on OOD retract actions; a conservative Q that is pessimistic off-support restores a usable, correctly-signed advantage so the retract-when-stuck preference can transmit. FIT: uses your existing replay + privileged asymmetric critic; TD-error prioritization dovetails with your PER lanes; deterministic-deploy native. EFFORT: medium (swap the critic/weighting objective). RISK: conservative critics can be over-pessimistic and stall improvement — tune the CQL alpha; still unimodal unless combined with candidate 2.

4) Hierarchy: discrete high-level option (ADVANCE / RETRACT-release-slack / REPOSITION) + continuous low-level control. Papers: multistage cable routing via hierarchical imitation learning (Luo et al., IEEE T-RO 2024) and "Hierarchical DLO Routing with RL + in-context VLMs" (arXiv:2510.19268, 2025); "HeRD" nonprehensile HL-DQN + LL-diffusion (arXiv:2512.10099) — HL commits to push-vs-navigate, converged ~16k samples earlier; RL follow-the-leader endoscopy (arXiv:2601.02798, 2026); needle-steering DRL WITH an explicit retraction mechanism (MDPI Processes 12(12):2852, 2024). MECHANISM: factoring the bimodal choice into a discrete high-level variable removes the averaging entirely — the Gaussian only has to execute ONE mode at a time; the RETRACT option is a first-class temporally-extended primitive (your "release slack then re-advance"). FIT: uses planned-path arclength as HL goal/subgoal signal; heuristic can bootstrap HL labels; sample-frugal. EFFORT: medium-high (option/termination plumbing). RISK: option discovery/credit assignment is finicky — mitigate by hand-defining the 3 options (they are physically obvious here) rather than learning them.

5) Model-based RL (Dreamer / TD-MPC2 world model) for SOFA sample efficiency. Paper: "World Model for AI Autonomous Navigation in Mechanical Thrombectomy" (arXiv:2509.25518, 2025) — latent-dynamics MBRL, references Hafner DreamerV3 and TD-MPC2, positioned for sample efficiency + safe planning under expensive-sim budgets. MECHANISM: learn a latent model of the wire/contact dynamics; plan short-horizon commit-vs-retract rollouts. FIT: strongest match to your 1-2M-steps/day SOFA constraint; planning can be deterministic at deploy. EFFORT: high (new stack, model of contact/buckling is hard). RISK: highest — modeling buckling/slack dynamics in latent space is exactly the regime where world models are least reliable; do AFTER 1-4.

CROSS-CUTTING (do regardless): put contact/force into the REWARD and (via distillation) into the policy, not only the critic. Best systems do this — CathSim Expert Navigation Network fuses visual+kinematic+force (Jianu/Huang et al., IEEE TMRB 2024, arXiv:2401.09059); safe two-device cerebral SAC uses a 0.85N force-penalty reward + LSTM + IRL demos, 96% on unseen vasculature, 0.24N mean force (Robertshaw et al., Int J CARS 2025, PMC12167253); Haptic-ACT force token -> adaptive retry 80% vs 50% vision-only. You have contact proxies/windup as privileged state used ONLY in the asymmetric critic — surface them into reward shaping for stall/buckling and into the deployable policy via stronger distillation.

## anti_recommendations
SKIP THESE despite surface appeal: (1) On-policy PPO/SAC from scratch or bigger entropy/alpha/mean-penalty tuning of the tanh-Gaussian — the entire cohort that succeeds on multimodal, recovery-heavy tasks has MOVED OFF unimodal Gaussians (ACT/CVAE, diffusion, B-spline transformer, hierarchy). Your 0.005-vs-0.02 mean-penalty knife-edge is a symptom of the wrong policy class, not a hyperparameter you can find. PPO/SAC-from-scratch are also the WORST performers in the domain (44-59%) and violate your SOFA budget. (2) Pivoting to image/fluoroscopy end-to-end obs — stEVE deliberately uses tracking/state obs so control quality isn't confounded by perception, and that is exactly the choice that transferred to real benches (97/100). You already hold the better-transferring representation; don't trade it for pixels. (3) Pure AWAC/advantage-weighted BC refinements — the advantage-collapse-to-1.0 you documented is the known AWAC failure when the critic is unreliable off-support; the field's offline winner (CASOG) replaced this with a CONSERVATIVE Q, not a better advantage temperature. (4) Reward-shaping-only campaigns — the 2025 systematic review explicitly warns "data quality is often more crucial than algorithmic sophistication"; your highest-leverage lever is labeled recovery data (DAgger + recovery demos), not another reward term. (5) World-model/Dreamer as the FIRST move — promising for your sample budget but it must learn buckling/slack contact dynamics in latent space, the regime where world models are least trustworthy; sequence it after the cheap IL/hierarchy wins. (6) Learning options from scratch for the hierarchy — the three options here (advance / retract-release-slack / reposition) are physically obvious; hand-define them rather than paying option-discovery credit-assignment cost.

## caveats
(1) No published system does your exact task — siphon-level multiple-180 deg-bend TWO-device autonomy with explicit slack/buckling recovery. The 40-59% comparators are for easier supra-aortic/bifurcation single-junction cases, so you may genuinely be AT the true frontier and the head-to-head is imperfect; treat "you're at SOTA" as directional, not a benchmarked tie. (2) Success rates come from different simulators (SOFA vs MuJoCo/CathSim), phantoms, and anatomies and are not directly comparable — the review itself states "few studies provide head-to-head comparisons under equivalent conditions." (3) The soft-robotic-IL recovery recipe (429 recovery demos, action chunking, relative actions, 83%) is on a soft MAGNETIC guidewire (different actuation) and a single deployable device, so the exact numbers won't transfer, though the principles (demonstrate recovery explicitly, chunk actions, use relative/hysteresis-aware outputs, multimodal head) are actuation-agnostic. (4) CASOG's PDF was not machine-readable in my fetch; its 94% / "backward steps" figures come from its abstract + the systematic review's summary, not the full methods — verify the CQL-alpha and prioritization details before adopting. (5) The world-model paper's (arXiv:2509.25518) concrete success numbers and sample-efficiency deltas were not cleanly extractable from the PDF; its advantage over SAC is asserted, not independently benchmarked here. (6) SplineFormer's 50% is a single-branch real-robot cannulation, not full deep navigation — do not read it as an end-to-end success rate. (7) I did not find any RL paper that models the buckling/slack hysteresis inside the policy dynamics; the recovery ideas are imported from needle steering, colonoscopy loop-reduction, and classical buckling-detect->retract reflexes, so their transfer to your FEM-slack regime is a reasoned extrapolation, not a demonstrated result.
