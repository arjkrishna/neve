# Cervical vertebral arteries must be extracted, not downloaded

No public dataset offers expert-labelled vertebral artery (VA) geometry along its cervical course (V1–V3) for more than a handful of subjects, and no multi-patient release labels the VA from its subclavian origin. After roughly 150 Zenodo queries and checks of TotalSegmentator, NVIDIA's MONAI/VISTA3D/NV-Segment models, the Grand Challenge and MICCAI releases, the Vascular Model Repository (VMR), TCIA, PhysioNet, OpenNeuro, Kaggle, Figshare and published dataset papers, only single anatomies qualify. **VMR `0248_H_AOCERE_CAS` is the one real, downloadable left-and-right VA pair that runs from the subclavian arteries to the basilar.** It is one CT patient with centerline paths, per-vessel wall surfaces and a 0.4 mm CT. The SPL Head-and-Neck Atlas adds a lower-resolution second subject, and BodyParts3D a low-poly template. Every labelled brain-vessel dataset stops at the skull base. TopBrain's VA labels come from images cropped to the braincase, which explains the 18–93 mm pieces already grafted, and TopCoW, TotalSegmentator, VISTA3D/NV-Segment, AortaSeg24, AVT and HaN-Seg have no VA class at all. Getting more than two or three anatomies therefore means extracting the VAs ourselves. The CC BY 4.0 TotalSegmentator v3 dataset holds about 71 head/neck CTAs and 52 neck-to-leg CTAs that image the whole VA and already carry subclavian, carotid and vertebra masks. The Arterial model, released on Zenodo in September 2026 under non-commercial terms, labels the right and left VA centerlines from the aortic arch to the basilar. Head-and-neck CTA corpora that label the VA do exist (HNCTA, 358 scans; HNA, 800), as does the 18,766-scan Fu et al. reconstruction set, but all of them are private or of unknown availability. The practical route is to graft VMR 0248 now, then run Arterial on the TotalSegmentator CTAs and check each result against the vertebral foramina and the released subclavian masks.

## Six open geometries include cervical VA, but only one is complete

**VMR `0248_H_AOCERE_CAS` is the only one of the 316 VMR projects whose anatomy spans the aortic root to the cerebral arteries.** It is a CT-derived "heart-to-brain" model of one UCHealth patient ("R-1"). Its 30 variants come from manually narrowing the carotid segmentations to stenoses of 10–100%, so the VA geometry is identical in all of them ([VMR metadata CSV](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SimVascular/vascularmodel/main/dataset/dataset-svprojects.csv)). The 717 MB reference ZIP downloads without a login. It contains SimVascular paths for the aorta, both subclavians, **`left_vertebral` and `right_vertebral`**, the carotids and the Circle of Willis segments. Alongside the paths are matching `.ctgr` contours, lofted per-vessel walls such as `wall_left_vertebral.vtp`, a volume mesh and the source CT `p12full.vti` (512×512×446 at 0.4×0.4×0.7 mm) ([0248 ZIP](https://www.vascularmodel.com/svprojects/0248_H_AOCERE_CAS.zip); [VMR file sizes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SimVascular/vascularmodel/main/dataset/file_sizes.csv)).

Measured from the extracted paths, **the left VA starts 1.3 mm from the left subclavian path and the right VA 4.2 mm from the right subclavian path.** The left path rises 20 cm and the right 18.4 cm. The left path continues through the basilar to the start of the P1 path, and the right path ends where it meets it ([0248 ZIP](https://www.vascularmodel.com/svprojects/0248_H_AOCERE_CAS.zip)). That is about twice the longest TopBrain piece now in the simulation (93 mm), so this single anatomy supplies the whole V0–V3 course that the host geometry currently fills in.

Two caveats apply. First, the 49-point control polylines are noisy: the left VA's arc length is 35 cm against its 20 cm vertical span. Lengths and radii should therefore be re-extracted with VMTK from the wall surfaces. Second, the aorta path lies below the bottom edge of the bundled CT, so the image may be cropped or come from a different series than the model. The VA origins do fall inside the CT. The licence permits free research and development use, provided the notice is kept and the NIH grants or Wilson et al. 2013 are acknowledged ([0248 ZIP LICENSE](https://www.vascularmodel.com/svprojects/0248_H_AOCERE_CAS.zip); [SDR deposit](https://purl.stanford.edu/qy363kk7638)).

**The SPL Head-and-Neck Atlas is the second real origin-to-upper-neck pair.** It comes from one CT (the OsiriX MANIX sample, reduced to 256×256) and ships `Model_105_right_vertebral_artery.vtk` and `Model_106_left_vertebral_artery.vtk`. They sit in the same frame as models of the subclavians, the aortic arch and the carotids, and the download is a 31.6 MB ZIP under the BSD-style 3D Slicer License ([Open Anatomy](https://www.openanatomy.org/atlas-pages/atlas-spl-head-and-neck.html)). The measured VA z-ranges start at −91.7 and −88.3 mm. Those values fall inside the subclavian ranges (−128.9 to −82.7 mm and −132.8 to −78.4 mm), so the VAs start at the subclavians. They then rise about 14 cm. No basilar model is listed, so it is unverified whether they reach the vertebrobasilar junction ([SPL atlas ZIP](https://www.openanatomy.org/atlases/nac/head-neck-2016-09.zip)). The reduced in-plane resolution makes the small VA lumen blocky, so smoothing and re-extracting the centerline is advisable.

The remaining open geometries are partial. **BodyParts3D/Anatomography 4.3** has "Trunk of right/left vertebral artery" meshes of about 800–1,100 faces, plus a basilar and both subclavians. That makes it a topological template rather than patient geometry, and its CC BY-SA 2.1 JP licence makes any redistributed derived meshes share-alike ([BodyParts3D 4.3 manifest](https://github.com/olivercase/body_parts_3d_api); [BodyParts3D README](https://dbarchive.biosciencedbc.jp/data/bodyparts3d/20110915/README_e.html)). **VMR 0050–0053** are four MR vertebrobasilar models of healthy adults aged 23–31. In 0050, the only one measured, both VA paths begin 1.5 mm above the bottom of the MR slab, so the field of view cuts them off. The left path spans about 9 cm, covering upper V2 through V4 without reaching the origin ([0050 ZIP](https://www.vascularmodel.com/svprojects/0050_H_CERE_H.zip)). **VMR 0018** is a paediatric coarctation case with only a 6.8 cm proximal VA stub ([0018 ZIP](https://www.vascularmodel.com/svprojects/0018_H_AO_COA.zip)).

Finally, a CC BY 4.0 **pseudo-angiographic FDG-PET atlas** (Zenodo 20307456) built from 42 volunteers has right and left VA labels that run 101–106 mm through the neck in MNI space. It is a single smoothed template whose lower end coincides with the lowest CCA slice, which points to a field-of-view cut-off rather than an anatomical landmark, and it never reaches V1 ([Zenodo 20307456](https://zenodo.org/records/20307456)). It is useful only as a population prior on the path.

## Arterial and 123 unlabelled TotalSegmentator CTAs can scale the count

Public images of the cervical VA exist; public labels do not. **The TotalSegmentator CT dataset v3.0.0 (1,939 CTs, CC BY 4.0, 37.4 GB, released 10 September 2026) contains 65 "ct angiography head", 6 "ct angiography neck" and 52 "neck-thx-abd-pelvis-leg" angiography studies** ([Zenodo 22688904](https://zenodo.org/records/22688904)). A remote probe of the ZIP read the headers and label extents of four CTA subjects. Three of them (s0292, s0152, s1112) run from the brachiocephalic trunk and subclavians at the bottom, through C1, to the skull vertex, so the entire VA is imaged. The fourth (s0744) was a head-only crop with no neck-vessel labels. The released masks include `subclavian_artery_left/right`, `common_carotid_artery_left/right`, `brachiocephalic_trunk`, the C1–C7 vertebrae and the skull. There is **no vertebral-artery, internal-carotid or jugular file** in any of the 1,939 subjects ([Zenodo 22688904](https://zenodo.org/records/22688904)).

The probed images are stored at 1.5 mm isotropic (not confirmed dataset-wide), so the VA lumen is only 2–3 voxels wide. That is enough for centerlines but coarse for radii, and native-resolution originals are not in the archive. The TotalSegmentator models themselves are Apache-2.0 and have no VA class in any task, CT or MR. `headneck_bones_vessels` adds the internal carotids, and `headneck_muscles` adds the scalenes and prevertebral muscles that border V1 ([TotalSegmentator README](https://github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator/blob/master/README.md); [class map](https://github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator/blob/master/totalsegmentator/map_to_binary.py)).

**Arterial is the only public tool found that labels the VA from origin to basilar.** Its Zenodo record (22694951, v1.0, 10 September 2026, 1.1 GB, "no account, no token and no licence gate") provides nnU-Net v2 weights for "vessel segmentation from the neck to the Circle of Willis" on head-and-neck CTA. A GNN then names the centerline segments ([Zenodo 22694951](https://zenodo.org/records/22694951)). Its 14 classes include **AA, RSA, LSA, RVA, LVA and BA**, so a correct run gives the arch → subclavian → vertebral → basilar chain directly ([Arterial vessel-labelling docs](https://github.com/FLOWCAT-CV/arterial/blob/main/docs/modules/vessel_labelling.md)). The outputs are NIfTI masks, VTK/STL surfaces and centerlines with radii ([FLOWCAT-CV/arterial](https://github.com/FLOWCAT-CV/arterial)).

Arterial has three limits. Its weights are CC BY-NC 4.0 and its code PolyForm Noncommercial. It was trained on acute-stroke CTA. And its training cohort and per-class VA accuracy are still a "TODO" in the README, so every VA it produces needs checking ([Zenodo 22694951](https://zenodo.org/records/22694951)).

NVIDIA offers a commercially licensed fallback but no VA class. VISTA3D, NV-Segment-CT and NV-Segment-CTMR learned their neck vessels (brachiocephalic trunk, common carotids, subclavians) from TotalSegmentator v2 pseudo-labels. Only **VISTA3D / NV-Segment-CT supports zero-shot point prompts**: you click foreground and background points and assign a free label index from 133 to 254. It is released under the commercial-friendly NVIDIA Open Model License ([NV-Segment-CT model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NV-Segment-CT); [VISTA3D paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05285)). The interactive branch was trained on SAM supervoxels, and no published accuracy on thin vessels was found. NV-Segment-CTMR has no point prompts and a non-commercial licence ([NV-Segment-CTMR model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NV-Segment-CTMR)).

Other open CTAs are weaker inputs. ISLES'24 (149 stroke cases, CC BY-NC-SA) released its CTAs "after cropping the images around the head, thus removing the thoracic region" ([ISLES'24 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.10966); [Zenodo 17652035](https://zenodo.org/records/17652035)). The uncropped `raw/` CTAs in TopCoW's external test set (26 ISLES'24-TUM cases) carry only Circle of Willis labels ([Zenodo 15692630](https://zenodo.org/records/15692630)). The 172-series head-CTA aneurysm set flags only which series include the carotid bifurcation ([Zenodo 15697196](https://zenodo.org/records/15697196)). None of these is verified to reach the VA origin.

## Every labelled brain-vessel dataset stops at the skull base

**TopBrain is the only public challenge with VA classes, and its crop rules out the neck.** Its right and left VA (labels 23/24) are shared by CTA and MRA across 25 training pairs ([TopBrain data page](https://topbrain2025.grand-challenge.org/data/); [Zenodo 16878417](https://zenodo.org/records/16878417)). The proposal states that the source CTAs "usually cover neck and head regions", yet "the defaced image includes only the braincase region" ([TopBrain proposal](https://zenodo.org/records/15084013)). The labels are therefore V4 plus at most the uppermost V3, which fits the 18–93 mm pieces already in use. The planned TopBrain 2.0 extends the label only to "V3 to V4 segments", still on brain-only crops ([TopBrain 2.0 proposal](https://zenodo.org/records/19707577)). The August 2026 36-class release adds infraclinoid ICA classes but no cervical VA ([Zenodo 21972006](https://zenodo.org/records/21972006)). TopBrain's written documentation gives no caudal boundary for the VA label, so the junction level has to be measured from the labels themselves.

Two of the exclusions still help at the host end. AortaSeg24 stops each branch at its first bifurcation, which for the left subclavian is likely near the VA origin. AVT (CC BY 4.0) covers "the aortic arch and its branches into the head/neck area" ([AVT paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8760499/)). Neither supplies the VA itself.

### Head-and-neck CTA VA labels exist only in private cohorts

The labelled data that would solve this problem is held by hospitals. **HNCTA** has 358 head-and-neck CTAs with nine vessel classes, including left and right VA, ICA, CCA and basilar, and its authors describe it as private ([DeformCL](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05820v1)). **HNA** annotates cerebral, vertebral and carotid arteries in 800 CTAs, with no public link ([arXiv 2301.02393](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.02393)). **Fu et al.** reconstructed vessels in 18,766 head-and-neck CTAs from five Chinese hospitals; their data-availability statement could not be retrieved ([Nat Commun 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-18606-2)). In-house work reports a 90.03% mean Dice for bilateral carotid and vertebral arteries on such data, so automatic cervical VA segmentation is tractable when labels exist ([LIVE-Net](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010482523003517)). The closest match in purpose is King's College London's thrombectomy-navigation work. It built 3D Slicer/VMTK meshes from 12 arch-to-cerebral CTAs, and later from 10, with no data release and no confirmed VA coverage ([arXiv 2503.24140](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24140); [arXiv 2509.25518](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25518)). Chinese-hosted repositories were not searched in Chinese, which is the main remaining blind spot.

## Graft VMR 0248 now, then extract dozens from TotalSegmentator

**Stage one uses what is ready.** Download the 717 MB VMR 0248 reference ZIP and take `wall_left_vertebral.vtp` and `wall_right_vertebral.vtp`, or the union model `Models/40Left85Right.vtp`. Re-extract the centerlines and radii with VMTK rather than trusting the noisy `.pth` polylines. Cut the graft proximally at the subclavian ostium and distally at the level where the TopBrain VA labels begin, which has to be measured from labels 23/24 in the TopBrain release because the documentation does not state it. The SPL atlas pair then gives a second, coarser cervical VA with its own subclavian and arch context.

**Stage two scales up with a verified extraction pipeline.** First, filter the TotalSegmentator v3 `meta.csv` for `ct angiography head` or `ct angiography neck` with 0.75 mm source slices. Include the 52 neck-to-leg CTAs, then pull only those subjects from the ZIP with HTTP range requests, as the probe did. Keep a subject only if its released masks include both subclavians, C1 and the skull, which rejects head-only crops like s0744 ([Zenodo 22688904](https://zenodo.org/records/22688904)). Next, run Arterial to get RVA/LVA centerlines joined to RSA/LSA and BA. Accept a VA only if its origin lies on the released subclavian mask, its V2 threads the C6–C1 transverse foramina in the vertebra masks, and its V3 loops around C1 toward the skull. Where Arterial fails, fall back to NV-Segment-CT point prompts or VMTK tracking seeded at the subclavian and at C1.

The two licences point in different directions. TotalSegmentator's CC BY 4.0 lets derived meshes be redistributed with attribution, but Arterial's non-commercial terms restrict use to research. A future commercial version would need the NV-Segment-CT or VMTK path.

The 1.5 mm voxels mean radii from this stage should be sanity-checked against the 0.4 mm VMR 0248 vessel. If the 3-of-4 hit rate from the probe holds, stage two yields dozens of anatomies rather than a handful. That brings in the variation in dominance, tortuosity and origin that a single patient cannot provide.

## Conclusion

The bottleneck is labels, not images. Public CTAs of the whole neck exist in CC BY 4.0 form, and the public labels stop at the skull base because of how the data was prepared, not because nobody imaged the neck. TopCoW and TopBrain cropped away necks their source scans contained, and even TopBrain 2.0 plans to stop at V3. For this project, VMR 0248 has a second use beyond being a graft. It is the only public case with expert-drawn VA paths from origin to basilar on a sub-millimetre CT. Running Arterial on `p12full.vti` and comparing the result with the SimVascular paths is a cheap, honest benchmark of the extraction pipeline before trusting it on 1.5 mm TotalSegmentator scans.

A small, QC'd set of cervical VAs extracted from CC BY 4.0 images would be redistributable. It would also fill a gap that, as far as these searches can tell, no open dataset covers today.
